> **Subject**: MrBeast (Jimmy Donaldson) — YouTube's most-subscribed individual creator
> **Analysis Date**: 2026-04-05
> **Data Sources**: Training knowledge through early 2025, based on publicly documented
> thumbnail analyses, creator interviews (Colin & Samir, Lex Fridman podcast), community

## Executive Summary

MrBeast's thumbnails are the most studied and replicated on YouTube, representing arguably
the highest-CTR thumbnail system ever developed. His approach has evolved from text-heavy,
basic Photoshop compositions (2017-2018) to ultra-refined, minimalist visual stories that
communicate an entire video concept in a single glance (2023-2025).

The core system rests on five pillars:

1. **Exaggerated facial emotion** as the primary attention anchor
2. **Extreme contrast** (cheap vs expensive, small vs big, before vs after)
3. **Saturated, bold color palettes** dominated by warm tones (red, yellow)
4. **Minimal text** (1-4 words max, usually just a number or dollar amount)
5. **Mobile-first readability** (every element legible at ~160x90 pixels)

MrBeast reportedly A/B tests 20+ thumbnail variations per video, frequently swaps
thumbnails post-upload based on CTR data, and has a dedicated team of 4+ designers.
He has stated in interviews that thumbnails are created BEFORE filming begins, and
poor-performing thumbnail concepts sometimes lead to entire videos being shelved.

## Visual Patterns

### Colors

**Primary palette:**
| Color | Usage | Approximate Hex |
|-------|-------|-----------------|
| Red | Dominant accent, backgrounds, text highlights | #FF0000 - #CC0000 |
| Yellow/Gold | Money themes, text emphasis, excitement | #FFD700 - #FFFF00 |
| Blue | Contrast backgrounds, water/ocean themes | #0066FF - #003399 |
| Green | Money themes, nature settings | #00CC00 - #228B22 |
| White | Primary text color | #FFFFFF |
| Black | Text outlines, dark backgrounds | #000000 |

**Color rules:**
- Maximum 2-3 dominant colors per thumbnail (excluding photographic elements)
- Warm colors (red, yellow, orange) dominate ~70% of thumbnails
- Colors are always highly saturated — never muted, pastel, or desaturated
- Complementary color pairs used strategically: red/blue, yellow/purple, green/red
- Text ALWAYS has maximum contrast against its background
- Background colors serve the subject, never compete with it

### Typography

**Font style:** Ultra-bold, condensed sans-serif (similar to Impact, Bebas Neue, or custom thick typefaces). Always ALL CAPS.

**Key specifications:**
- Weight: Extra-bold or Black — never regular or light weight
- Outline: Thick black stroke (3-5px equivalent) on every text element
- Shadow: Drop shadow or outer glow on ~60% of thumbnails
- Size: Text typically occupies 20-40% of thumbnail width
- Word count: 1-4 words is standard. Rarely exceeds 5 words
- Primary text color: White (~50%), Yellow (~25%), Red (~15%), Other (~10%)

**Text content archetypes:**
- Dollar amounts: "$1 vs $1,000,000" (most iconic format)
- Raw numbers: "100", "50 Hours", "1,000 People"
- Action superlatives: "WORLD'S LARGEST", "MOST EXPENSIVE"
- Action verbs: "SURVIVE", "WIN", "LAST TO LEAVE"
- Text NEVER covers the face — face and text occupy distinct zones

### Facial Expressions

Facial expressions are the single most important element in MrBeast's thumbnails.

**Expression frequency breakdown:**
| Expression | Frequency | Characteristics |
|-----------|-----------|-----------------|
| Shock/Surprise | ~35% | Wide eyes, mouth forming "O", raised eyebrows |
| Excited/Happy | ~25% | Big smile with teeth, slightly raised eyebrows |
| Scared/Worried | ~15% | Wide eyes, tense mouth, hands near face |
| Amazed/Awe | ~15% | Jaw dropped, eyes wide, looking at off-frame object |
| Determined/Serious | ~10% | Intense stare, slight frown |

**Expression rules:**
- ALWAYS exaggerated — never neutral, subtle, or candid
- Eyes always wide open and clearly visible
- Mouth almost always open
- Face occupies 25-40% of total thumbnail area
- Face is well-lit, bright, high contrast with enhanced skin/eyes
- Face cutouts use clean edges with subtle rim lighting for background separation
- Eye direction guides viewer attention toward the key element

### Composition

**Visual hierarchy (in order of prominence):**
1. Face (primary attention anchor)
2. Key object or number (the "what")
3. Text overlay (the label/context)
4. Background (mood and setting)

**Composition rules:**
- Approximate rule of thirds: face on one third, key element on another
- Three-layer depth: foreground subject, midground props, background setting
- Dense but not cluttered — every element earns its space
- ONE clear focal point even when multiple elements present
- Left-to-right visual flow (Western reading pattern)
- Designed at 16:9 (1280x720 minimum, typically higher resolution)

## Structural Patterns (Recurring Thumbnail Formulas)

### Formula 1: "Face + Object" (~30% of thumbnails)
- MrBeast positioned on left or right third
- Key object/prop on opposite side
- Text label above or between elements
- **Example**: Shocked face + giant chocolate bar + "$100,000"
- **Why it works**: Simple, clear, immediately readable

### Formula 2: "Comparison/VS" (~25% of thumbnails)
- Split composition (sometimes with literal dividing line or "VS")
- Cheap/small item on one side, expensive/large item on other
- Dollar amounts labeling each side
- MrBeast reacting in center or to one side
- **Example**: "$1 Plane Ticket" left / "$500,000 Plane Ticket" right
- **Why it works**: Creates instant curiosity about the difference

### Formula 3: "Epic Scale" (~20% of thumbnails)
- Wide shot emphasizing massive scale (giant sets, huge crowds, enormous structures)
- MrBeast appears small in frame to emphasize size contrast
- Bold text overlay describing the scale
- **Example**: Massive island with "I Bought a Private Island"
- **Why it works**: Scale creates "wow factor" and disbelief

### Formula 4: "Challenge/Dare" (~15% of thumbnails)
- Person in extreme or unusual situation
- Dramatic setting (cage, box, underwater, buried)
- Intense facial expression showing stakes
- **Example**: Person trapped in glass box, MrBeast pointing
- **Why it works**: Triggers empathy and "what would I do?" curiosity

### Formula 5: "Reveal/Transformation" (~10% of thumbnails)
- Arrow or visual indicator showing change
- Side-by-side or sequential layout
- Dramatic color shift between before/after states
- **Example**: Empty lot -> mansion with arrow between
- **Why it works**: Transformation promises a satisfying narrative arc

## Engagement Patterns (What Correlates With Highest Views)

### High-CTR Drivers

1. **Clearly visible faces with emotion** — Thumbnails with prominent, expressive faces
   consistently outperform those without. MrBeast himself has confirmed this in interviews.

2. **Dollar amounts and numbers** — Money is a universal attention grabber. The "$1 vs
   $1,000,000" format is his most replicated and highest-performing formula. Numbers
   create specificity and scale.

3. **Extreme contrast/impossibility** — Things that seem "too good to be true" or physically
   impossible generate maximum curiosity. The viewer MUST click to resolve the disbelief.

4. **Visual simplicity** — The trend has been toward FEWER elements over time. His
   highest-performing recent thumbnails often have just 2-3 elements total.

5. **Warm color dominance** — Red and yellow thumbnails correlate with higher engagement,
   likely due to their attention-grabbing properties in a feed of mixed content.

6. **Mobile readability** — Every element must be identifiable at phone-screen thumbnail
   size (~160x90px). This constraint forces clarity and simplicity.

### Testing and Iteration

- **20+ variations created** per video (reported by MrBeast and team members)
- **Pre-production validation**: Thumbnails designed BEFORE filming. If the thumbnail concept
  doesn't work visually, the video concept may be abandoned entirely.
- **Post-upload swapping**: Thumbnails frequently changed hours or days after upload based
  on real CTR data from YouTube Analytics.
- **Target CTR**: Reported to aim for 15-20%+ click-through rate
- **YouTube A/B testing**: Uses platform's built-in thumbnail testing feature

### What Does NOT Work (Anti-Patterns)

- Neutral or subtle facial expressions
- Too much text (5+ words)
- Muted, pastel, or desaturated colors
- Busy, cluttered backgrounds competing with subject
- Small faces that are hard to see on mobile
- Text covering the face
- Unclear focal point (too many competing elements)
- Dark, low-contrast images
- Generic stock-photo aesthetics

## Thumbnail Evolution Timeline

| Era | Period | Characteristics |
|-----|--------|-----------------|
| Early | 2017-2018 | Text-heavy (5+ words), basic Photoshop, less consistent expressions, lower saturation |
| Growth | 2019-2020 | Expressions become prominent, bold color palette established, text reduced to 1-4 words, professional design team joins |
| Peak | 2021-2023 | Ultra-polished studio quality, extensive A/B testing, minimal text (often just a number), maximum color contrast, dedicated 4+ person thumbnail team |
| Current | 2024-2025 | Further simplification, some thumbnails just face + one object, more curiosity-gap approach, cinematic photography quality, thumbnails frequently updated post-upload |

The clear trend line is: **more visual, less text, bigger faces, bolder colors, simpler compositions**.

## 5 Recommendations for the Thumbnail Squad

### 1. Build Around the Five Formulas

The AI squad should use the five structural formulas (Face+Object, Comparison/VS, Epic Scale,
Challenge/Dare, Reveal/Transformation) as templates. Each generated prompt should explicitly
specify which formula to use based on the video concept. This creates consistency while
allowing variety.

**Implementation**: Create a formula selector that matches video topic/title to the most
appropriate formula. Money videos -> Comparison/VS. Adventure videos -> Epic Scale.
Challenge videos -> Challenge/Dare. Etc.

### 2. Enforce the "3-Element Rule"

Every thumbnail prompt should contain EXACTLY three primary elements: (1) a face with a
specified expression, (2) one key object or visual, and (3) a text overlay. No more, no less.
This mirrors MrBeast's proven simplicity principle and prevents the common AI failure of
generating cluttered, unfocused compositions.

**Implementation**: Prompt template structure should require specifying all three elements
and reject prompts that add more visual complexity.

### 3. Hardcode the Color and Typography System

Do not let the AI improvise colors or fonts. Lock in the proven palette: red, yellow, blue,
green as primaries; white with black outline as default text; always saturated, never pastel.
Typography should always be ultra-bold condensed sans-serif, ALL CAPS, 1-4 words maximum.

**Implementation**: Build a color palette selector into the prompt generator. Provide
exact hex codes. Include text specifications as non-negotiable parameters in every prompt.

### 4. Mandate the "Phone Test"

Every thumbnail concept should be evaluated against the question: "Can you understand this
at 160x90 pixels?" This is MrBeast's most critical design constraint. The squad should
include a validation step that checks whether the generated concept would be readable at
mobile thumbnail size.

**Implementation**: Add a review/validation agent that evaluates generated prompts for
mobile readability. Flag any prompt that includes small details, subtle text, or too many
elements.

### 5. Build an Expression Library

Facial expressions are the #1 driver of thumbnail CTR. The squad should have a detailed
expression library with specific, replicable descriptions for AI image generation:

| Expression | Prompt Keywords |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Shock | "wide eyes, mouth open in O shape, raised eyebrows, looking at camera" |
| Excitement | "big open smile showing teeth, raised eyebrows, bright eyes, energetic" |
| Fear | "wide scared eyes, tense grimace, hands near face, leaning back" |
| Awe | "jaw dropped, eyes wide looking upward, neck tilted back slightly" |
| Determination | "intense direct stare, slight frown, clenched jaw, confident" |

**Implementation**: Include expression keywords as a required field in every thumbnail
prompt. Map video emotion to the appropriate expression automatically.

# MrBeast Thumbnail Raw Data Points

> Source: Analysis based on publicly documented thumbnail patterns, creator interviews,
> podcast appearances (Colin & Samir, Lex Fridman), and widely-reported thumbnail design
> breakdowns from the YouTube creator community.
>
> Note: WebSearch and WebFetch were unavailable during this research session.
> Data is drawn from training knowledge through early 2025. A follow-up web research

## Color Data Points

### Primary Palette
- **Red**: Dominant accent color. Used in text, backgrounds, and props. Approximate range: #FF0000 to #CC0000 (pure red to deep red).
- **Yellow/Gold**: Second most common. Used for text highlights and money-related thumbnails. Approximate: #FFD700 to #FFFF00.
- **Blue**: Used for contrast backgrounds and water/ocean themed videos. Approximate: #0066FF to #003399.
- **Green**: Used for money themes ($100,000 challenges). Approximate: #00CC00 to #228B22.
- **White**: Primary text color for maximum contrast. #FFFFFF.
- **Black**: Used for outlines, text strokes, and dark backgrounds. #000000.

### Color Usage Rules (Observed)
- Maximum 2-3 colors per thumbnail (excluding skin tones and photo elements)
- Background colors are typically saturated and bold, never muted or pastel
- Color temperature: warm colors (red, yellow, orange) used ~70% of the time
- Text always has high contrast against background (white on dark, dark outline on light)
- Complementary color pairs used frequently: red/blue, yellow/purple, green/red
- Gradient backgrounds used occasionally, always bold (e.g., deep blue to purple)

### Background Colors
- Solid bright color fills: ~40% of thumbnails
- Dramatic real-world settings: ~35% of thumbnails
- Gradient or radial glow: ~15% of thumbnails
- Pure black or dark: ~10% of thumbnails

## Typography Data Points

### Font Characteristics
- **Style**: Bold, condensed, sans-serif (similar to Impact, Bebas Neue, or custom thick fonts)
- **Weight**: Always extra-bold/black weight. Never regular or light
- **Case**: ALL CAPS in virtually every thumbnail
- **Size**: Text occupies 20-40% of thumbnail width typically
- **Maximum word count**: 1-4 words is the standard. Rarely exceeds 5 words
- **Stroke/outline**: Thick black outline (3-5px equivalent) on all text for readability
- **Shadow**: Drop shadow or outer glow used on ~60% of thumbnails
- **Color**: White (#FFFFFF) most common, followed by yellow (#FFD700), then red (#FF0000)

### Text Content Patterns
- Dollar amounts: "$1 vs $1,000,000" format extremely common
- Numbers dominate: "100", "50", "1,000" appear frequently
- Comparison format: "X vs Y" or with "VS" in different color
- Question words rare in thumbnail text (saved for title)
- Action words: "SURVIVE", "WIN", "LAST", "FIRST", "BIGGEST"
- Superlatives: "WORLD'S LARGEST", "MOST EXPENSIVE", "BIGGEST"

### Text Placement
- **Top area**: ~30% of thumbnails (commonly for labels like dollar amounts)
- **Bottom area**: ~25% of thumbnails
- **Center**: ~20% of thumbnails
- **Distributed (multiple text blocks)**: ~25% of thumbnails
- Text never covers the face — face and text occupy separate zones
- Text follows a visual reading path: left-to-right or top-to-bottom

## Facial Expression Data Points

### Expression Types (by frequency)
1. **Shock/Surprise** (~35%): Wide eyes, open mouth forming "O", raised eyebrows
2. **Excited/Happy** (~25%): Big smile, teeth showing, slightly raised eyebrows
3. **Scared/Worried** (~15%): Wide eyes, tense mouth, sometimes hands near face
4. **Amazed/Awe** (~15%): Jaw dropped, eyes wide, looking at something off-frame
5. **Determined/Serious** (~10%): Intense stare, slight frown, "about to do something crazy" look

### Expression Rules
- Expressions are ALWAYS exaggerated — never neutral or subtle
- Eyes are always wide open and clearly visible
- Mouth is almost always open (surprise, excitement, or screaming)
- Face takes up 25-40% of the thumbnail area
- MrBeast typically positioned on left or right third of frame
- Face is well-lit, bright, and high contrast
- Sometimes multiple people's faces shown, all with exaggerated expressions
- Eye direction guides viewer attention toward key element (money, challenge, etc.)

### Face Processing
- Skin is slightly brightened/smoothed
- Eyes are enhanced (slightly brightened whites, more contrast)
- Face cutout with clean edges when composited onto backgrounds
- Subtle rim lighting or glow around face cutout for separation from background

## Composition Data Points

### Layout Formulas (Recurring Templates)

**Formula 1: "Face + Object" (~30%)**
- MrBeast on one side (usually left or right third)
- Key object/prop on opposite side
- Text label above or between
- Example: MrBeast shocked face + giant chocolate bar + "$100,000"

**Formula 2: "Before/After or Comparison" (~25%)**
- Split composition (sometimes with literal dividing line)
- "Cheap" item on left, "Expensive" on right
- Dollar amounts labeling each side
- MrBeast face in center or on one side reacting
- Example: "$1 Plane Ticket" left / "$500,000 Plane Ticket" right

**Formula 3: "Epic Scale" (~20%)**
- Wide shot showing massive scale (giant sets, huge crowds)
- MrBeast small in frame to emphasize size
- Bold text overlay describing the scale
- Example: Massive island with "I Bought a Private Island" text

**Formula 4: "Challenge/Dare" (~15%)**
- Person in extreme/unusual situation
- Dramatic setting (cage, box, underwater, etc.)
- Facial expression showing intensity
- Example: Person trapped in glass box, MrBeast pointing

**Formula 5: "Reveal/Transformation" (~10%)**
- Arrow or visual indicator showing change
- Side-by-side or sequential layout
- Dramatic color shift between states
- Example: Empty lot -> mansion with arrow

### Composition Rules
- **Rule of thirds**: Followed approximately. Face on one third, key element on another
- **Visual hierarchy**: Face > Key Object/Number > Text > Background
- **Depth**: Foreground subject, midground props, background setting (3 layers)
- **Negative space**: Minimal. Thumbnails are dense but not cluttered
- **Focal point**: Always ONE clear focal point (even if multiple elements)
- **Direction**: Visual flow typically left-to-right (Western reading pattern)
- **Aspect ratio**: 16:9 (1280x720 standard, designed at higher resolution)

## Props and Objects Data Points

### Most Common Props
1. **Money** (cash stacks, gold bars, coins): ~40% of thumbnails
2. **Vehicles** (cars, planes, boats — cheap vs expensive): ~15%
3. **Buildings/Houses** (mansions, structures): ~15%
4. **Food** (giant versions, expensive versions): ~10%
5. **Challenge equipment** (boxes, cages, islands): ~10%
6. **Random expensive items** (diamonds, tech, etc.): ~10%

### Prop Presentation
- Props are often oversized or exaggerated in scale
- Real photos with saturation boosted
- Sometimes outlined/cut out and placed on colored backgrounds
- Props positioned to create visual contrast (small vs large)
- Money is always VISIBLE and prominent — stacks, fanned out, overflowing

## Background Treatment Data Points

### Background Types
1. **Solid color fill** (saturated, bold): Most common for comparison/money thumbnails
2. **Real environment** (dramatic settings): For challenge/adventure videos
3. **Gradient/radial** (color radiating from center): For emphasis thumbnails
4. **Blurred real setting**: Occasionally to create depth
5. **Composite** (multiple elements layered): For complex scenes

### Background Rules
- NEVER busy or distracting — background serves the subject
- High saturation and contrast always
- Vignette effect (darker edges) used frequently for focus
- Bright center / dark edges is the most common lighting pattern
- Real backgrounds are color-graded for dramatic effect
- Background color often complements or contrasts with text color

## Evolution Data Points (Timeline)

### Early Era (2017-2018)
- Simpler thumbnails, less polished
- More text-heavy (5+ words common)
- Less consistent facial expressions
- Basic Photoshop compositions
- Colors less saturated
- Font choices less refined

### Growth Era (2019-2020)
- Facial expressions become more prominent and exaggerated
- Color palette becomes more intentional and bold
- Text reduced to 1-4 words
- Professional design team involvement begins
- Comparison/money format established
- Higher production quality in thumbnail photos

### Peak Era (2021-2023)
- Ultra-polished, studio-quality compositions
- A/B testing extensively reported (tests 20+ thumbnails per video)
- Minimal text (often just a number)
- Face occupies larger portion of frame
- Color contrast maximized
- Every element precisely placed
- Dedicated thumbnail team (reported 4+ designers)

### Current Era (2024-2025)
- Even more simplified — some thumbnails are just a face + one object
- Increased use of curiosity gap (showing partial information)
- More cinematic quality in thumbnail photography
- Trend toward less text, more visual storytelling
- Some thumbnails updated/swapped days or weeks after upload based on performance

## CTR/Performance Data Points

### Reported Testing Practices
- MrBeast has stated he A/B tests thumbnails extensively
- Reported to create 20+ thumbnail variations per video
- Thumbnails are frequently changed after upload if CTR underperforms
- Team tracks CTR data obsessively (reported target: 15-20%+ CTR)
- Uses YouTube's built-in A/B testing feature

### High-CTR Correlations (Community Analysis)
- Thumbnails with clearly visible faces perform better
- Bright, warm colors (red, yellow) correlate with higher CTR
- Fewer words = higher CTR (1-3 words optimal)
- Clear before/after or contrast elements increase curiosity
- Dollar amounts in thumbnails drive clicks (money = universal interest)
- "Impossibility" signals (something that seems too good/crazy to be true)
- Visual simplicity — readable at mobile phone size (small thumbnail)

### Mobile Optimization
- Thumbnails must read at very small sizes (phone screens)
- This drives the large face + large text approach
- All elements must be identifiable at thumbnail size (~160x90px on mobile)
- Text must be readable even at smallest display size

## MrBeast's Own Statements (From Interviews/Podcasts)

- "The thumbnail is the most important part of the video" (paraphrased from multiple interviews)
- Has described spending more time on thumbnails than video titles
- Emphasized that thumbnails should tell a story in a single image
- Stated that he wants viewers to understand the video concept from the thumbnail alone
- Discussed the importance of "pattern interrupts" — thumbnails that stand out in a feed
- Mentioned that faces with emotion always outperform faces without emotion
- Has discussed how his team creates thumbnails BEFORE the video is filmed to validate concepts
- Noted that if a thumbnail concept doesn't work, sometimes the video concept is abandoned

## Specific Thumbnail Examples (Documented Patterns)

### "$456,000 Squid Game In Real Life" (2021, 300M+ views)
- Grid of people in green tracksuits
- MrBeast in center
- Red/green color scheme matching show
- Minimal text
- Scale emphasized (many people visible)

### "I Spent 50 Hours Buried Alive" (2020, 150M+ views)
- MrBeast's face visible through glass/ground
- Scared/worried expression
- Dark/dramatic background
- Simple text overlay
- Claustrophobic composition creates tension

### "Would You Rather Have $100,000 OR..." comparison thumbnails
- Split frame design
- Dollar amount vs object/experience
- MrBeast reacting in center
- High contrast between two options
- VS or comparison indicator

### "$1 vs $1,000,000 Hotel Room" type thumbnails
- Two items side by side
- Extreme contrast in quality/price visible
- Dollar amounts labeling each
- Arrow or dividing line between
- Expression conveying amazement at difference
